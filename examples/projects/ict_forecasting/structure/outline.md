# THESIS

ICT energy forecasts diverge by twenty-fold because each embeds untested assumptions rather than because the underlying physics disagrees.

# A. Context: forecast divergence and its consequences [role: introduction]

  - Global ICT electricity demand by 2030 ranges from 800 TWh to over 16,000 TWh across published forecasts [ref: andrae_edler_2015, masanet_2020, malmodin_2018]
  - Capacity planning, grid investment, and climate accounting all depend on which forecast is right [user_synthesis] [supports: thesis]
  - MY VIEW: this divergence is not a measurement problem but an assumption problem [user_synthesis] [role: setup]
  - Figure 1: forecast spread 2020 to 2030 [central_contribution]

# B. The forecast landscape [role: argumentative] [depth: deep]

  - Stabilisation camp: Masanet, Malmodin, IEA assume continued efficiency gains offset traffic growth [ref: masanet_2020, malmodin_2018, iea_2025] [role: setup]
  - Explosion camp: Andrae projects 2,800 TWh for wireless networks alone by 2030 [ref: andrae_edler_2015] [role: evidence]
  - Visibility critique: Mytton and Ashtine show most public estimates trace to non-peer-reviewed sources [ref: mytton_ashtine_2022] [role: complication]
  - MY VIEW: the divergence between camps is driven by different assumptions about efficiency, not by different observations of energy use [user_synthesis] [role: conclusion] [supports: thesis]

# C. Gap 1: Untested efficiency assumptions [role: argumentative] [depth: deep]

  - Stabilisation forecasts assume Koomey's Law continues at historical pace [user_synthesis] [role: setup]
  - Koomey's Law doubling period lengthened from 1.5 years to 2.6 years across the 2010s [ref: koomey_2015] [role: evidence]
  - Esmaeilzadeh and colleagues identify Dennard scaling breakdown around 2006 as the mechanism [ref: esmaeilzadeh_2011] [role: mechanism]
  - COUNTER: accelerator-era architectures partially recover the trajectory [user_synthesis] [weak] [role: complication]
  - Landauer's thermodynamic limit exists but is far from binding today [ref: landauer_1961] [role: limit]
  - MY VIEW: efficiency trajectory is the highest-leverage unresolved question in the forecast literature [user_synthesis] [role: conclusion] [supports: thesis]

# D. Gap 2: Suppressed demand and infrastructure constraints [role: argumentative]

  - Most forecasts treat infrastructure availability as exogenous [user_synthesis] [role: setup]
  - Coleman documents 2025 offshore processing growth as a response to grid constraints [ref: coleman_2025] [role: evidence]
  - Meta's Hyperion lease structure illustrates how grid access shapes deployment geography [ref: meta_hyperion_2025] [role: evidence]
  - Suppressed demand from grid constraints could mask 20 to 40 percent of underlying energy intensity [user_synthesis] [role: synthesis]

# E. Gap 3: Capital depreciation and the AI capex cycle [role: argumentative]

  - Burry's Scion Asset Management filing in November 2025 highlights AI infrastructure depreciation pace [ref: burry_scion_2025] [role: evidence]
  - Average lease life of 5.7 years for hyperscale data centre equipment implies write-down acceleration [ref: burry_scion_2025] [role: mechanism]
  - Forecasts that price energy from capital costs underestimate this depreciation drag [user_synthesis] [role: conclusion]

# F. Discussion: a path to convergence [role: discussion]

  - The gaps identified above are independent and additive [user_synthesis] [role: synthesis]
  - A new metric, Productive Resource Efficiency (PRE), would surface assumption sensitivity directly [user_synthesis] [role: conclusion] [supports: thesis]
  - Future research should construct PRE for each major forecast and identify which assumptions drive the spread [user_synthesis] [role: conclusion]

# G. Conclusion [role: conclusion]

  - The twenty-fold spread in published ICT forecasts reflects differences in assumption, not differences in observation [user_synthesis] [role: conclusion]
  - Closing this gap requires assumption-explicit forecasting, not more granular measurement [user_synthesis] [role: conclusion] [supports: thesis]
